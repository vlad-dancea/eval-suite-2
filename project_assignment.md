# Project Description
In this project, you will develop a Proof of Concept in groups of up to 4 people, present your results on a poster, gather feedback from your peers, and incorporate this feedback into your final hand-in.

For the exact timeline see the Schedule on TUWEL.

## Work on your project
You will build 2 main artefacts: a poster and a well documented proof of concept (software) for your project. We expect that you spend 100h per group member purely on this project (excluding the poster sessions as well as the test and test preparation). When grading the scope of your final submission, we will take your group size into account. For the ECTS workload breakdown take a look at the preliminary lecture (slides and recording available).

### Poster Specifications
The poster must consist of 3 vertically stacked A3-landscape-pages. It must be submitted as a single top-down-ordered PDF (i.e. page 1 = top most A3 section, page 2 = middle A3 section, page 3= bottom most A3 section).

The poster must present the following concepts:

- Title of the project

- Group Number

- Group Members

- Abstract: A short, concise summary of the project

- Initial Situation / Problem: What problem are you addressing / what are the questions you aim to answer?

- Approach: How does your system work? E.g. architecture diagram, key design decisions, what models/tools you used. (This should be the visual centrepiece)

- Results / Demo: What does the PoC actually do? Screenshots, example inputs/outputs, or key metrics if applicable.

- Learnings & Limitations: What worked, what didn't, what would you do differently?

We are aware of the fact that depending on the poster session date you have made different progress on your project. This poster should present the current status. It's totally fine if key parts are still in progress – especially if you are in one of the early slots. However, we still expect that you have already worked on at least some key aspects and therefore can present them in the poster session.

You are free to present a demo of your product on your own device. Whether you have a demo or not will not be graded; we just want to give you the opportunity to present it if you think it helps understand your project better.

### Poster Session
Each poster session will be split up into two rounds. In the first round (12:00-13:00), roughly half of the groups present their posters while the other half walks around as audience. In the second round (13:00-14:00), groups swap – first-round presenters become audience and the first-round audience presents their own posters.

There are no formal presentation slots. Sessions are held in a "marketplace" format where posters are displayed on stands and groups stand next to their poster. Fellow students and the lecture team walk freely between posters. Every group member must be able to explain the full project, as we may ask anyone any question about it. The lecture team will spend approximately 5-15 minutes per poster.

Each student must provide written feedback to 3 other posters. The posters/groups do not have to be from your own 2h-session, but since you're already there, that is usually the easiest option. Feedback is submitted via TUWEL (details TBA) and due by the end of the poster-session.

Attendance is required during the complete session (12:00-14:00). Attending other sessions is encouraged but not mandatory. Keep in mind there won’t be a stream or recording of the poster sessions.

### Final Submission
Alongside your poster, you are required to submit the actual Proof of Concept (software) you built. You must submit a single ZIP file on TUWEL containing:

1. Your complete Proof of Concept source code: Include a single, concise README.md with clear step-by-step instructions on how to set up and run the project. Your code must be runnable on our laptops. Avoid hardcoded file paths, expose configuration either via your user interface or in a single, clearly documented config file. Avoid OS-specific setups. If this is truly not possible, you must provide a link to a hosted version of your software in addition to the code.
Do not include installed dependencies (e.g. node_modules/, .venv/). Your README.md should reference the relevant package manager commands (e.g. pip install -r requirements.txt, npm install). For credentials, use environment variables or a clearly marked config file (e.g. .env.example) that we can fill in ourselves.

2. A short video (screencast) where you walk us through your project

3. A concise CHANGES.md listing what feedback you received, during the poster session, what you changed in response and why; and if you chose not to incorporate specific feedback, briefly explain your reasoning.

4. Your final poster (same format as the poster session submission). This should be your revised version incorporating feedback from the poster session.

## Tech Stack
You are free to use whatever tech stack you want, given that we can run the system on our own devices or you provide a hosted version for us to evaluate. You may choose any of those interfaces for your project:

- Web application

- Cross-platform desktop application

- Browser plugin/extension

- Unix-based terminal application

- Jupyter Notebook

- Others upon request (send us an email with your proposal and justification).

### The Project Test
As announced in the preliminary discussion the 2nd test will be about your project. Further details will be announced at a later point in time.

### Evaluation of your project
As announced in the preliminary discussion you can earn up to 80 points during the project phase:

- Poster Submission & Presentation (20 Points): Graded on the overall quality and clarity of the poster, your ability to explain your project, and technical execution, scope / complexity of the project as well as the creativity of the solution.

- Poster Session Feedback (5 Points): Full points are awarded for submitting constructive, written feedback for 3 posters in time. There are partial points (e.g. providing feedback to only one or two posters, or failing to give valuable feedback).

- Final Project Work Submission (35 Points): Graded on technical execution, scope / complexity of the project, creativity of the solution, the clarity of your documentation and how well you incorporated (or justified rejecting) feedback from the poster session.

- Test about the project (20 Points)
